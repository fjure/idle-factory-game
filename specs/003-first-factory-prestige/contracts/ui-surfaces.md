# Contract: UI Surfaces and States

**Consumers**: UI/UX Developer, independent UI/UX Reviewer, Art Director, Test/QA.
**Normative for**: FR-011, FR-032–FR-034, SC-007 and the player-visible parts of US1–US5.

## Global rules (carried from Feature 001 and FR-032/FR-033)

- **Supported screen classes**: the existing matrix `360x640`, `390x844` and `768x1024`, each with its rotated form, plus desktop `1280x720` and `1920x1080`. The existing `FactoryHudLayout` classes apply (phone, tablet portrait, tablet landscape, desktop). Safe areas are respected, and layout works in panel points, never pixels (wiki: UI Toolkit HUD layout pitfalls).
- **Device profile** (owner decision 2026-10-07, T139 finding F-10; implemented by T191 in `ShellDeviceProfile`, `ShellPanelScale` and `RunShellDocument.ApplyDeviceProfile`): the layout class follows the panel-point size, so a landscape phone may get the tablet-landscape or desktop layout; the phone-landscape side sheet applies only where the panel-point size yields the phone class. Separately, the shell knows the device: primary input (touch or pointer/keyboard; a mobile platform, or a touch screen without mouse or keyboard, is touch), screen size in pixels, DPI where `Screen.dpi` is known (0 is unknown) and orientation.
  - **Touch behaviour follows the profile, not the layout class**: on a touch profile a tap pins the exact-value detail line and a 500 ms press shows it, and hover shows nothing; on a pointer profile hover and focus show it, and a mouse click or press never pins (a touch or pen press still does, for hybrid screens).
  - **Physical size**: on a touch profile with a known DPI the shell raises the panel scale to a constant pixel scale of `dpi x 7 / 25.4 / 44` when the authored scale is lower, so a 44 pt control is at least 7 mm. The raise is applied to the live `PanelSettings` at runtime and restored when the shell is torn down, so the authored `CoreGameFoundationPanelSettings` asset (scale with screen size, 1280x720, match 0.5) stays unchanged. A pointer profile keeps the authored scale. A touch profile with an unknown DPI also keeps it; that fallback is recorded (`ScaleDecision.Reason` is `UnknownDpiFallback` and the shell logs it once). Example: 1080x2400 at 420 dpi raises the scale to 2.63 px per pt, so the panel is 410x912 pt (Compact portrait) or 912x410 pt (Compact landscape, with the side sheet); the same device as a pointer profile keeps 2.09 (portrait) or 1.69 (landscape) px per pt, and its landscape form is 1422x640 pt, which classifies as Desktop. The authored scale blends the width and height ratios linearly, so it differs by orientation.
  - **Test seam**: `ShellDeviceProfile.Create/Touch/Pointer/Detect` build a profile without a device; `RunShellDocument.AutoProbeDevice = false` stops the shell from probing; `ShellRig.Create(screen, profile, textScalePercent)` and `ShellRig.CreateAuthored(...)` in `RunShellTestSupport.cs` apply a profile and a text scale and build a panel with the authored settings at a device's pixel size.
- **Text scale** (T139 finding F-4; FR-032, Art Bible 9.3; implemented by T191 in `ShellTextScale` and `FactoryRun.uss`): one shell-owned factor from 100 % to 200 %, from the Android system font scale (`Configuration.fontScale`, clamped to 1.0 to 2.0, read at start and every 2 s) and 100 % elsewhere; there is no in-game setting in this feature. The shell puts one class `run-text-scale--NNN` (NNN = 100, 110, ... 200, the factor rounded to 10 %) on its root. At 100 % the minimum text is 12 pt; at 200 % no text is clipped or truncated in meaning: from 120 % the top strip and the tab bar wrap into rows (three tabs per row), tab labels and the panel title may wrap, the sheet scrolls, the zoom buttons sit side by side, and on a phone in landscape from 150 % the side sheet is 40 % wide.
  - **Panel rule**: a panel never sets a fixed `font-size`. It uses `font-size: var(--run-fs-caption)` (12 pt at 100 %: units, hints, tab labels), `var(--run-fs-body)` (14 pt: labels, panel text, buttons) or `var(--run-fs-value)` (16 pt: Cash, RP and store numbers), declared in a rule of the panel's own style sheet or in `FactoryRun.uss`; `var(--run-fs-zoom)` is the zoom glyph. Panels mounted under the shell root inherit the variables. A control keeps `min-height: 44px` and no fixed `height` (text may grow it), a text node does not use `white-space: nowrap` unless a layout test proves it fits at 200 %, and a panel that needs a different layout at large text selects with `.run-text-scale--200` (and the other steps) as a descendant selector.
  - **How to test at 200 %**: build the panel into `ShellRig.Create(screen, null, 200)` for every screen class (`ShellScreens.All()` includes the rotated forms), with worst-case content, and call `RunShellLayoutAssertions.AssertShellLayout(rig, state)`; it asserts no overlap, no squeezed or horizontally clipped text, 44 pt targets and a scaled 12 pt minimum for the rig's text-scale step. Also run `AssertScrollContentClipped` on the sheet scroll view and focus the first, middle and last control to prove it scrolls into view. `RunTextScaleTests` is the reference (100, 150 and 200 % at every screen class).
- **Controls**:
  - Every actionable control is at least **44×44 points**, activatable by touch and mouse, and reachable and activatable from the desktop keyboard with a visible focus ring.
  - A clipped control scrolls into view when it receives focus.
  - Text scales, and no state is conveyed by color alone (icon, text or shape plus color).
- **Density** (UX-1): on phones the HUD top strip holds Cash, RP once unlocked, the store usage bar and the Overdrive control; the bottom tab bar holds the six tabs at 44 pt or more each. A bottom sheet is at most 60 % of the safe-area height and the selected slot stays in view above it. In phone landscape (360 pt tall) sheets become side sheets of at most 50 % width. On tablet and desktop the Overdrive control sits in the side-panel header. `RunHudLayoutTests` asserts these rules at every screen class.
- **Focus** (UX-2): every confirmation uses the shell's shared dialog. It traps focus, opens with focus on Cancel, returns focus to the invoking control on close, and treats Escape and Android Back as Cancel. Left and right arrows move along the tab bar. After a rotation cancel, focus returns to the invoking control. `RunKeyboardFocusTests` covers the dialog, and each panel's tests cover its own confirmations (Remove, Prestige, Start new run, Item activation, Abandon).
- UI-owned pointers stay UI-owned. A world gesture never activates a panel control, and a panel press never pans the world.
- **Rotation** cancels any open confirmation, drag-to-reorder or pending selection without spending, placing or confirming prestige, and it creates no offline summary (wiki: Android runtime rotation). A one-line notice "Cancelled by rotation" tells the player what happened (UX-9).
- **Rejections**: every rejected command shows its reason code's copy next to the control that was used, and the state stays unchanged. Affordability is shown before the tap (disabled with the reason visible), and the command is still re-validated.
- **Numbers**: compact display is floored (R06). The exact value shows on focus or hover, and on touch through a tap that expands a detail line (UX-8). Long-press (500 ms) is an extra shortcut only on elements that are neither draggable nor part of the world, so it never conflicts with pan or drag-to-reorder.

## Evidence required (QA and UI/UX review)

- A Play Mode test per surface for normal, empty, disabled and error states, reached through application seams without editing the scene.
- A layout test at every screen class asserting that there is no overlap, that clipping is contained and that the focused control is visible (extending `HudLayoutOverlapTests` and `ZoomReadabilityTests`), for the shell and for each panel with worst-case (maximum) content, including the rotated classes (UX-3).
- Keyboard traversal: every control is reachable, and Enter/Space activates it.
- Touch-target measurement of at least 44 pt on every actionable element.
- An early independent UI/UX review of the shell before the panels start (T139, UX-4) and the final independent UI/UX Reviewer record, both in `reviews/ui-ux-review.md`, citing the screen, state and acceptance scenario.
- Visual tokens (palette roles, focus ring, minimum text size, disabled look, contrast) come from the Art Bible's UI token section (ART-4).
